When something breaks in production, the goal is to restore service as quickly as possible — then understand why it happened. This section covers everything you need to respond confidently: severity definitions, a step-by-step response process, on-call expectations, and the postmortem workflow.

## Severity levels

Use these levels to calibrate your response speed and who you pull in.

| Level | Description | Response target |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| SEV-1 | Production down or data loss | Immediate, all hands |
| SEV-2 | Significant degradation, major feature broken | Within 30 minutes |
| SEV-3 | Minor degradation, workaround available | Within 2 hours |
| SEV-4 | Cosmetic or low-impact issue | Next business day |

## Responding to an alert

<Steps>
  <Step title="Acknowledge the alert">
    Acknowledge in your alerting tool to signal you're on it and stop repeat pages going to others.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Assess severity">
    Determine whether this is SEV-1/2 (needs immediate action) or SEV-3/4 (can be handled at a measured pace).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Open a war room">
    For SEV-1 or SEV-2, create a Slack thread in `#incidents` and invite your on-call partner. This becomes the central coordination point.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Mitigate first">
    Prioritize restoring service before diagnosing root cause. Roll back, disable a feature flag, or scale up resources — whatever stops the bleeding fastest.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Communicate regularly">
    Post updates to `#incidents` every 15 minutes until the incident is resolved, even if there's nothing new to report. Silence creates anxiety.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Resolve and document">
    Mark the incident resolved in your alerting tool. For SEV-1 and SEV-2, file a postmortem within 24 hours.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Mitigating first — before you fully understand the cause — is the right call. A rollback now is better than a perfect fix in two hours.
</Note>
