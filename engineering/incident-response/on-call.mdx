On-call responsibility rotates weekly among engineers on each team. Being on-call means you're the first responder for production alerts during your shift — the person who acknowledges the page, triages the issue, and either resolves it or escalates. This page covers what's expected of you before, during, and after your shift.

## Rotation schedule

Check your team's on-call schedule in NightWatch. Schedules are set four weeks in advance. If you need to swap a shift, arrange it directly with a teammate and update the schedule in NightWatch — don't just not show up.

<Note>
  New engineers join the rotation after 60 days. Your first few on-call weeks are shadowed — you respond, but a senior engineer is on secondary so you have backup if you get stuck.
</Note>

## Before your shift

Make sure you're ready before your shift starts:

- Confirm your phone number is correct in the alerting tool.
- Check `#deploys` for any recent changes that might be fragile or need extra attention.
- Review any open SEV-3 issues that could escalate during your shift.

## During your shift

**Availability:** Respond to pages within 5 minutes during business hours, and within 15 minutes outside business hours. If you can't respond within that window, escalate to secondary immediately — don't wait.

**For each alert:**

1. Acknowledge the alert to stop re-paging.
2. Triage: is this a real issue or noise? If it's noise, resolve the alert and file a ticket to fix the alerting rule.
3. If it's real, follow the [incident response process](/engineering/incident-response).

**Keeping notes:** Maintain a running Slack thread in `#incidents` even for small issues. It creates a record and helps the next on-call engineer understand what happened during your shift.

## Escalation path

If you've been investigating for 15 minutes and are stuck, escalate — don't sit on it:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Page the secondary on-call engineer">
    Use the escalation path in NightWatch to page your secondary. Brief them quickly on what you've found so far.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Escalate to your engineering manager">
    If you and secondary are still stuck after another 15 minutes, escalate to your engineering manager. They can pull in additional resources or make product decisions.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Asking for help quickly is the right call. Engineers who escalate early resolve incidents faster than those who wait until they're really stuck.
</Tip>

## After your shift

Hand off any open issues to the next on-call engineer in `#incidents`. Include: what happened, the current status, and what to watch for during their shift.

## On-call compensation

On-call shifts outside of business hours are compensated. See HR or your manager for current rates.
