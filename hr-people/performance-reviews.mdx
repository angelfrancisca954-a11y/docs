We run two performance review cycles each year: a mid-year review in July and an annual review in January. Understanding how each cycle works — and what's expected of you — helps you get the most out of the process and walk into review conversations prepared.

## Review cycles

The **annual review** (January) informs compensation adjustments. This is where merit increases and promotion decisions are made. The **mid-year review** (July) is developmental — no compensation decisions are made, and the focus is entirely on growth and feedback.

Mid-year follows the same structure as the annual cycle, compressed into two weeks.

## Timeline

| Month | What happens |
|-------|-------------|
| December | Self-assessment opens |
| January (week 1) | Peer feedback collection |
| January (week 2–3) | Manager writes reviews |
| January (week 4) | Review conversations |
| February | Compensation adjustments communicated |

## Self-assessment

You'll fill out a self-assessment in the HR portal before your review. The questions cover:

- Key accomplishments from the review period
- Areas where you grew
- Areas you want to develop going forward
- Anything your manager should know that may not be visible in day-to-day work

Be specific. Vague answers make your manager's job harder and often result in weaker, less actionable feedback. Concrete examples give reviewers the evidence they need to advocate for you.

## Peer feedback

You'll nominate 3–5 peers to provide feedback. Your manager may add or adjust the list. Peer feedback is confidential — reviewers are not identified in your written review.

When you're the reviewer, give honest and specific feedback. Comments like "great to work with" don't help the recipient grow or give managers useful signal.

## The review conversation

Your manager will share your written review with you before the meeting. Read it in advance, come with questions, and be prepared to share your reactions. The conversation should cover three things: what went well, what to work on, and what success looks like in the next period.

## Ratings

We use a five-point scale to assess performance.

| Rating | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 5 — Exceptional | Consistently exceeded expectations, had outsized impact |
| 4 — Strong | Met and often exceeded expectations |
| 3 — Meets expectations | Solid, reliable performance |
| 2 — Developing | Some gaps; an improvement plan may follow |
| 1 — Not meeting expectations | Significant performance concerns |

<Note>
  Most employees fall in the 3–4 range. A rating of **3 (Meets expectations)** is a genuinely good outcome — it means you are doing your job well and meeting the bar for your level. It is not a warning sign or a cause for concern.
</Note>
