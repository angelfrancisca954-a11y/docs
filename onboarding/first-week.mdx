Your first week is about building context, not proving yourself. You'll have a clear picture of your team's priorities, have spoken with every direct teammate, and ideally shipped something small — enough to understand how work moves from idea to done.

## Goals for the Week

By end of week one you should have:

- All accounts active and tools installed
- One 1:1 completed with your manager
- Introductions with every member of your immediate team
- A rough picture of current team priorities
- A project or workstream to focus on in week two

<Info>
  These goals are a floor, not a ceiling. If you move faster, great — but don't skip the relationship-building steps to get there. Those conversations compound.
</Info>

## Recommended Schedule

<Steps>
  <Step title="Day 1 — Complete the first-day checklist">
    Work through every item in the [first-day checklist](/onboarding/first-day) before moving on. Your accounts, laptop, and VPN all need to be functional before you can do anything else effectively.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Days 2–3 — Team introductions">
    Book 20-minute slots with each of your direct teammates. Keep them informal. Good questions to ask: what are you working on right now, what slows you down most, and what should I know about how this team operates?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Day 4 — First 1:1 with your manager">
    Come with questions. A useful framing to bring up: what does success look like at 30, 60, and 90 days? This is also a good time to ask which work is most impactful for you to pick up first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Day 5 — Reflect and write it down">
    Spend time on Friday writing down what you learned, what is still unclear, and what you want to tackle first in week two. Sharing this with your manager is optional but often appreciated.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Keep a running note of questions that come up through the week. Batching them into your 1:1 on Day 4 is more efficient than sending individual Slack messages for each one.
</Tip>
