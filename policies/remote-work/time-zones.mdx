We have team members across multiple time zones, and making that work well requires intentional defaults. Not everyone needs to be online at the same time — but we do need clear norms around when to expect responses, how to schedule meetings fairly, and how to signal your availability to colleagues who may be wondering why you've gone quiet.

## Async-First

Async-first means the default expectation is that people will respond when they're working, not immediately. A message in Slack is not an interruption that demands an instant reply. Assume your colleagues are focused and will get back to you within their working day.

If something is genuinely urgent and time-sensitive, call it out explicitly — for example, "need this by EOD PT" — or ping directly with context. Don't use urgency language for things that aren't actually urgent.

## Core Overlap Hours

Teams establish a window of overlap hours where synchronous meetings and real-time collaboration are expected. Check with your manager for your team's current overlap window. Outside those hours, async is the default and you're not expected to be immediately available.

## Scheduling Meetings

- Share your working hours in your calendar and Slack status so colleagues know when you're reachable.
- Use a scheduling tool (e.g., Calendly) or share multiple time options that span different time zones.
- Rotate meeting times for recurring meetings — don't consistently schedule at times that fall outside someone's working hours.
- Record video meetings when possible so people who can't attend can catch up on their own time.

<Tip>
  Keep your Slack status up to date throughout the day. A quick "🎧 heads down until 2pm ET" or "🌴 OOO today" gives your colleagues the context they need without interrupting you for a status check. It's one of the simplest things you can do to be a good distributed teammate.
</Tip>

## Communication Expectations

| Channel | Expected Response Time |
|---------|----------------------|
| Slack DM | Within your working day |
| Slack `@channel` / `@here` | Within your working day |
| Email | Within 24 hours on a working day |
| `@urgent` tag | As soon as reasonably possible |

Do not set an expectation of faster response for non-urgent messages. If something is genuinely time-sensitive, say so explicitly — otherwise, assume standard response times apply.

## Out-of-Office and Availability

Update your Slack status when you're heads-down, out sick, or on vacation. Use your calendar's out-of-office feature for absences longer than a day so that meeting invites are automatically declined.

This is especially important on a distributed team — if you're heads-down and won't respond for a few hours, a quick status update prevents colleagues from worrying or escalating unnecessarily.

## Working Hours Flexibility

We don't require fixed 9–5 hours. You're responsible for meeting your commitments and being available during your team's core overlap window. How you structure the rest of your working day is up to you.

<Info>
  Flexibility is a privilege that comes with accountability. If your availability or output is affecting your team, your manager will work with you to find a schedule that works for everyone.
</Info>
